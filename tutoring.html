Why This Exists

Two things happened at once. The curriculum was rebuilt, and peer tutoring was cut.

That combination matters more than either change alone. In a normal year, the informal support system carries a lot of the weight — the upper-year who hands you their study guide, the warning about which block breaks people, the sense of what's worth memorizing. A curriculum change wipes most of that out. Old exams don't map. Study guides go stale. Faculty are teaching these blocks for the first time too.

So this is the year with the least informal guidance available and no formal tutoring to replace it. That's the gap this fills — honestly, and without pretending to certainty nobody has yet.

Veterinary Students Helping Veterinary Students

Kyle B.
DVM Student · Founder, desmoVet

I sat in weekly group tutoring sessions as a first-year. They're a large part of why that year went as well as it did. Later I became the coordinator of that program, and got to watch the same thing happen for a lot of other students.

This year the program was cut. The reasoning was that students should get through it on their own — and some will. But starting veterinary school is one of the hardest transitions there is, and going through it during a full curriculum rebuild is harder still. That's not the year to remove the safety net.

So I'm keeping peer tutoring alive independently. Same idea that helped me, run properly, with tutors paid fairly for their time — because asking students to teach for free is how these programs quietly die.

Veterinary students helping veterinary students. That's the whole thing.

Start with a group session. Most first- and second-year sticking points are shared by half the class, and hearing someone else ask the question you were embarrassed to ask is often the whole unlock. Move to one-on-one when you need something specific.

Where the Line Is

⚠️
Tutors help you understand material. They never produce work you turn in.

No help with graded assignments. No discussion of exam content. No sharing of lecture slides, recordings, or question banks. If you're stuck on a graded problem, your tutor builds a similar one and works through that with you — you learn the method and complete your own assignment.

This service is independent and is not affiliated with or endorsed by any veterinary college. Your institution's honor code applies to you in full. Read the full policy before your first session — it takes three minutes and it protects you.

Questions

The curriculum just changed. Has my tutor actually taken my course?

No — and neither has anyone else. We'd rather say that up front than have you discover it in session one.

Here's what that does and doesn't change. It does mean no one can tell you how this specific block is assessed, what the exam format will be, or which lecturer emphasises what. Anyone claiming otherwise this year is guessing.

It doesn't change the material. Renal physiology is renal physiology whether it arrives in week 6 or week 20, and the places people get stuck — countercurrent multiplication, acid–base compensation, autonomic pharmacology — are remarkably consistent across curricula and across decades. Your tutor has learned this content, been examined on it, and in most cases has since applied it to actual patients. That last part is what a textbook can't give you.

There's also an upside worth naming: because we work with students across both years and every subject, we see the pattern of where the new curriculum is actually causing trouble faster than any individual student can. That gets shared back.

Is this allowed?

Yes. Tutoring is a normal part of academic life, and this service is independent of any school — no school space, materials, or branding is used. What isn't allowed is help with graded work or exam content, and tutors are contractually required to refuse it. If you're ever unsure about a specific request, ask before your session rather than after.

Who are the tutors?

Veterinary students in the years ahead of you who did well in that subject. For the clinical material — pharmacology, anesthesia, large animal medicine — tutors are typically third- and fourth-years who have since used it in clinics, which changes how well someone can explain why any of it matters.

Every tutor completes a teaching assessment before being accepted. Knowing the material and being able to teach it are different skills, and we screen for the second one. Students rate every session, and tutors who fall below standard don't stay.

Will anyone find out I'm using this?

Not from us. We don't discuss your performance or your use of this service with classmates, faculty, or administrators, and tutors sign a confidentiality obligation covering the same. Asking for help early is what strong students do.

What if I don't click with my tutor?

Tell us and we'll match you with someone else at no charge. Tutoring is personal and not every pairing works — it's not awkward and you don't need to explain. What we can't do is refund a session that's already happened; your tutor did the work and gets paid for it, the same as any other professional service.

How do group sessions work?

Four to six students, one hour, one topic, $15 each. You can bring your own study group or we'll place you with others working on the same material. Sessions run with a minimum of four people and seats are prepaid, so we know it's going ahead.

If a session doesn't reach four, we'll move you to the next one or refund the seat — you're never charged for a session that didn't run.

Most people should start here. It's a third of the price, and the questions other people ask are usually the ones you didn't know to ask.

Can you help me pass?

We can help you understand the material faster and study more effectively. Nobody honest will promise you a grade — the exam is still yours to write, and anyone guaranteeing an outcome is selling you something.

How far ahead should I book?

Sooner than feels necessary. Demand spikes in the two weeks before every major exam, and that's exactly when availability disappears. One session in week three beats three sessions the night before.

How is this different from the Cardio and Neuro Tutors?

Those are free interactive study tools you work through on your own — they're on the Tools page and always will be free. This page is one-on-one tutoring with an actual person. The two are connected: desmoVet's share of each paid session goes back into building and expanding those free tools.

Is this a business or a student service?

Both, and we'd rather be straightforward about it than pretend otherwise. Tutors are paid because unpaid peer tutoring collapses the moment people get busy — that's roughly what just happened to the program this replaces. desmoVet Peer Tutoring is operated by KB Enterprises LLC, the same entity behind the free tools on this site, and the service's share is spent running it and expanding those tools.

I'm an upper-year student. Can I tutor?

Probably — we're recruiting. $36/hour, you set your own availability, no minimum commitment, and matching, scheduling, and payment are handled for you. Email tutoring@desmovet.com.
